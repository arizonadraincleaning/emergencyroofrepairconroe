What a Conroe Storm Does to Your Roof

Conroe sits in the I-45 severe-weather corridor and takes two to three damaging hail events in a typical year. In April 2026, hail up to three inches wide crossed roughly 76 square miles and put more than 9,700 Montgomery County homes in the direct path. In July 2024, Hurricane Beryl pushed 69 mph gusts and five inches of rain across the county. Storm damage here is rarely a single leak; it is dozens of small impact points that fail together weeks later.

The damage a hail or wind event leaves behind is specific, and an adjuster looks for exactly these signatures:

Hail Bruising & Granule Loss

Round impact marks knock protective granules off the shingle mat, exposing the asphalt to UV and shortening roof life. Bruises are soft to the touch and often invisible from the ground.

Wind-Lifted & Creased Shingles

Straight-line gusts break the adhesive seal along ridges and rakes, leaving a crease line where the shingle folded back. Sealed-down again, it still leaks under the next driving rain.

Dented Flashing, Vents & Gutters

Soft metal on vents, valleys, and gutters records hail size clearly, and adjusters use these dents to date and size the storm that caused your claim.

• WHY WAITING COSTS YOU

The 12-Month Claim Window

Texas insurers generally require a storm claim to be filed within one year of the date of loss. Every month you wait, the damage looks less like "sudden storm damage" and more like "wear and tear," which is the easiest reason for an adjuster to deny it. A free documented inspection now protects the claim later.

ACV vs RCV, in plain English:

An ACV (actual cash value) policy pays the depreciated value of your old roof. An RCV (replacement cost value) policy pays to put a brand-new roof back, minus your deductible, and releases the held-back depreciation once work is done. We help you read which one you have before the adjuster arrives.

• REPAIR OR REPLACE

When Storm Damage Means a Full Replacement

Not every storm roof is a replacement, and we will tell you honestly when a targeted repair is enough. But Texas code includes the widely used 25% rule: if more than a quarter of the roof is damaged within a 12-month span, it generally must be brought fully up to current code rather than patched. After a major Conroe hail event, a properly documented roof often clears that threshold, which is why a spot repair and a full insurance-covered replacement can cost you the same deductible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Conroe Storm Damage & Insurance FAQs

Do I have to pay anything if my storm damage is covered?
On a covered claim you pay only your policy deductible. On a replacement cost (RCV) policy, your carrier covers the rest of the approved scope, releasing held-back depreciation once the work is finished and invoiced.
How long after a Conroe hail storm can I still file a claim?
Texas insurers generally require claims within one year of the date of loss, but sooner is far better. Older damage is easier for an adjuster to classify as wear and tear and deny, so a dated inspection right after the storm protects you.
Can you tell if my roof was hit if I cannot see damage from the ground?
Yes. Most hail bruising is invisible from the ground and only shows on a hands-on roof inspection of test squares, soft metals, and vents. Our free inspection documents it with photos and chalk marks the adjuster recognizes.
Do you handle the insurance company for me?
We prepare the documentation and meet your adjuster on the roof to align the scope and Xactimate pricing. You file the claim with your carrier; we make sure every piece of covered damage is on the estimate, including supplements the first pass missed.
